The Cognitive Sovereignty Act

Official bill text, whitepapers, and lobbyist resources.

Section 1: Definitions

For the purposes of this Act, the following definitions apply.

1.1 Cognitive capture

Cognitive capture means the process by which an individual’s behavioral data or decision-making patterns are collected, inferred, or used to influence that individual’s choices or outcomes without their ongoing, informed consent, or in a manner that undermines their ability to exercise meaningful agency over their own cognition and behavior.

1.2 Severance

Severance means the technical and legal separation of personally identifiable or re-identifiable data from the systems and processes that make decisions affecting an individual, such that the individual’s identity cannot be inferred from the data used in those processes, and such that the individual retains sovereignty over the use of their own data for behavioral or cognitive inference.

1.3 High-dimensional data

High-dimensional data means data that, in combination or in context, can be used to infer, model, or predict an individual’s behavior, preferences, mental state, or identity, including but not limited to biometric, behavioral, and contextual data.

1.4 Cognitive Agency Review Board (CARB)

Cognitive Agency Review Board or CARB means the body established or designated under this Act to set and enforce severance levels, audit compliance, and certify systems and practices that meet the standards of cognitive sovereignty set forth herein.

Section 2: Findings and purpose

The legislature finds that:

(1) The increasing use of high-dimensional data and automated systems to infer or influence individual behavior poses a threat to individual autonomy, privacy, and the integrity of human decision-making.

(2) Technical and legal safeguards are necessary to ensure that individuals retain sovereignty over their own cognitive and behavioral data, and that such data is not used to capture or manipulate them without their informed consent.

(3) A clear standard of severance—whereby data used in decision-making systems cannot be linked back to identifiable individuals without their consent—advances both innovation and fundamental rights.

Purpose. The purpose of this Act is to establish a framework for cognitive sovereignty by requiring severance between personally identifiable data and systems that make or support decisions affecting individuals, and by empowering a Cognitive Agency Review Board to set and enforce severance levels and related standards.

Section 3: Rights and standards

3.1 Right to severance

An individual has the right to have their high-dimensional or behavioral data processed under a regime of severance when such data is used in systems that make or support decisions affecting them, such that their identity is not inferable from the data used in those systems without their consent or a lawful exception.

3.2 Severance levels

The Cognitive Agency Review Board shall define and publish severance levels (including such levels as may be designated S, V, M, C, and X or equivalent) and shall certify systems and practices that meet those levels. Use of an individual’s data in a manner that does not meet the applicable severance level may be prohibited or restricted as provided by rule or by further legislation.

3.3 No-Trace and minimal logging

Systems that process high-dimensional or behavioral data in connection with this Act shall, where applicable, adhere to No-Trace or minimal-logging practices such that prompt content and personally identifiable information are not retained in provider or third-party logs beyond what is strictly necessary and legally permitted.
